# Efron, 1998 — R. A. Fisher in the 21st century

**Authors:** B. Efron
**Year:** 1998
**Venue:** Statistical Science, vol. 13, no. 2, pp. 95–122 (invited paper, 1996 R. A. Fisher Lecture)
**arXiv:** n/a
**DOI:** 10.1214/ss/1028905930

## One-paragraph summary
Efron's R. A. Fisher Lecture: a retrospective on Fisher's contributions and a speculation about which Fisherian ideas remain alive in modern statistics. Discusses the survival of likelihood, ANOVA, MLE asymptotics, and conditional inference, and explicitly returns to fiducial inference as an idea that — while wrong in its strongest form — survives in the modern guise of objective Bayes, confidence distributions, and matching priors. Famously calls fiducial inference "Fisher's biggest blunder" but also predicts that something fiducial-flavoured will return.

## Why CD-SBI cites it
Historical context in §1.2 (claim ID **D-CD historical**). Specifically, Efron 1998 is the standard retrospective citation for the modern revival of fiducial/CD-style thinking after a long dormancy following the criticisms of the 1950s–60s. The CD-SBI manuscript pairs it with Efron 1993 as the bridge between Fisher's original fiducial argument and the Schweder–Hjort synthesis.

## Specific anchors
- §§7–8 of Efron 1998 contain the fiducial-inference retrospective and the "biggest blunder" / "may yet return" remark.
- §2 contextualizes Fisher's broader programme.

## Notes
- This is a *historical / interpretive* citation, not a technical one. It does not contribute mathematics that CD-SBI uses. Its function in §1.2 is to give the reader a single accessible entry point into the modern view that fiducial-style inference is worth revisiting in a frequentist framework.
