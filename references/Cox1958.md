# Cox, 1958 — Some problems connected with statistical inference

**Authors:** D. R. Cox
**Year:** 1958
**Venue:** Annals of Mathematical Statistics, vol. 29, no. 2, pp. 357–372
**arXiv:** n/a
**DOI:** 10.1214/aoms/1177706618

## One-paragraph summary
An invited address (Princeton, April 1956) ranging across several foundational issues in statistical inference. The most cited contribution is the *two measuring instruments* thought experiment — if a coin flip selects between a precise and an imprecise instrument and the choice is observed (an *ancillary* statistic), one's inference should be conditional on the instrument actually used rather than averaged over the random choice. This is the canonical motivating example for the *conditionality principle* and for conditional inference on ancillary statistics, and is one of the historical roots of the conditional / Fisherian view of frequentist inference. Cox also discusses significance tests, the relation between hypothesis tests and decision theory, and the interpretation of likelihood.

## Why CD-SBI cites it
- §1.2 (historical lineage of CDs): cited together with Fisher 1930 and Efron 1993/1998 as part of the Fisher–Cox–Efron–Schweder–Hjort line that the modern CD synthesis builds on. Claim ID **D-CD (historical)**.
- §5.3 (location families / Theorem t-loc): cited for the ancillary-statistic definition that the proof there invokes.

## Specific anchors
- §§2–3 of Cox 1958 contain the two-instruments example and the formulation of the conditionality principle.
- The ancillary-statistic discussion is the relevant piece for §5.3 of the CD-SBI manuscript.

## Notes
- Cox 1958 is not really a "confidence distribution" paper — its connection to the CD lineage is via conditional inference and the broader conditional / Fisherian tradition that the CD synthesis inherits. The §1.2 citation is appropriate as historical context but should not be read as Cox having proposed CDs.
- The §5.3 use (ancillary statistic) is the more technically load-bearing of the two.
