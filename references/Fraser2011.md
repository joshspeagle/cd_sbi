# Fraser, 2011 — Is Bayes posterior just quick and dirty confidence?

**Authors:** D. A. S. Fraser
**Year:** 2011
**Venue:** Statistical Science, vol. 26, no. 3, pp. 299–316 (with discussion)
**arXiv:** 1112.5582
**DOI:** 10.1214/11-STS352

## One-paragraph summary
Fraser argues that a Bayesian posterior, integrated against a "default" or matching prior, often functions as a first-order approximation to a frequentist confidence distribution — but only to first order, and only when the model is locally linear in the parameter. In nonlinear models the approximation can break down badly: nominal posterior credibility levels can differ substantially from actual frequentist coverage. The paper is a forceful frequentist critique of the casual use of Bayesian posteriors for inferential statements about parameters and is widely cited as the canonical statement of the "posterior ≠ confidence" position. Followed by a multi-discussant exchange in the same issue.
