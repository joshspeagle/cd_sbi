### D-UMNN
**Verdict:** ⚠ under-justified
**Quote (lines 1251–1264):** "A *Unconstrained Monotonic Neural Network* parameterizes a scalar function `g(z; c)` that is strictly monotone-increasing in `z` ... `g(z; c) = bias(c) + ∫_{z_0}^z softplus(MLP(t, c)) dt` ... The integrand is positive everywhere (softplus is positive), so `g` is strictly monotone in `z` by construction. The derivative `∂_z g(z; c) = softplus(MLP(z, c))` is closed-form ... We use Gauss–Legendre quadrature (`n = 12` nodes by default)."
**Diagnosis:** The mathematical content of the definition is correct: softplus > 0, so the *exact* integral defines a strictly increasing function with closed-form derivative softplus(MLP(z,c)) by the fundamental theorem of calculus. The under-justification is the slippage between *the exact UMNN* (where strict monotonicity and the derivative identity hold) and *the implemented UMNN under n=12-node Gauss–Legendre quadrature*. The quadrature returns a polynomial-in-z approximation of the integral; its derivative (the *true* `∂_z g` of the trained, deployed model) is *not* softplus(MLP(z,c)) but the derivative of the polynomial. In practice the manuscript uses softplus(MLP(z,c)) as the Jacobian factor in the NF-MLE loss. This is fine *only* if the quadrature error is small enough that (a) g remains strictly monotone (true when quadrature error in `∂g` is < softplus's positive value, which it is for n=12 on smooth softplus(MLP) integrands), and (b) the gap between the closed-form derivative and the true derivative of the quadrature-evaluated forward map is negligible at the loss-and-diagnostic scale. The statement as written implies these are mathematical equalities; they are quadrature-level approximations. For a methodology paper that hinges on (R2) being enforced *architecturally*, this is worth a clarifying sentence — because the §8.4 ablation argument turns on the difference between architectural and autograd Jacobians.
**Suggested fix:** Add a half-sentence after "closed-form": "(In implementation, the integral is evaluated by 12-node Gauss–Legendre quadrature; the quadrature error in g is below 10^{-X} on the integrand class used here, and we use the analytical softplus(MLP(z,c)) as the Jacobian factor in NF-MLE — see §7.2.)" Strict monotonicity of the *deployed* g is also implied because the quadrature is a positive linear combination of softplus values evaluated at the nodes, so the quadrature itself is monotone in the integration limit.

### C-7.3-noisefloor
**Verdict:** ✓ holds
**Quote (lines 1319–1325):** "Under the null, `√N · KS` has the Kolmogorov limiting distribution with `Pr(√N·KS > 1.628) = 0.01`. At N = 5000, this gives a noise floor of `KS ≈ 1.628/√5000 ≈ 0.023` for the 0.01-level threshold; at N = 6000, the floor is ≈ 0.021."
**Diagnosis:** The Kolmogorov critical-value claim is correct: K(1.628) ≈ 0.99 (two-sided Kolmogorov–Smirnov 99th percentile is 1.628 to three sig figs; K(1.36) ≈ 0.95 is the canonical 95th-percentile companion). Arithmetic: 1.628/√5000 = 1.628/70.71 ≈ 0.02303 → 0.023 (rounded). 1.628/√6000 = 1.628/77.46 ≈ 0.02102 → 0.021. Both round correctly. The two-sided KS test is standard for testing whether a sample comes from a fixed reference distribution (the use case here, since the reference is `U(0,1)` or `N(0,1)` or `χ²_d`, all fully specified — not estimated), so the standard Kolmogorov distribution applies. "0.01-level threshold" is read naturally as "false-positive rate 0.01 under the null", which is the right interpretation. Note: the Kolmogorov limit holds asymptotically; at N = 5000 the limit is essentially exact (the asymptotic Berry-Esseen-type rate makes the finite-sample correction far below the third significant figure used here). No issues.
**Suggested fix:** None.

### C-7.3-diagnostics
**Verdict:** ⚠ under-justified
**Quote (lines 1288–1317):** "1. Pivot RMSE vs `r*` ... 2. Marginal PIT ... 3. Conditional PIT ... 4. Joint Mahalanobis (`d>1`) ... 5. Coverage."
**Diagnosis:** Three concerns, none fatal but each worth a sentence.
(a) *Logical redundancy among diagnostics 2 and 3.* If conditional PIT (`r_k(θ_0,X)|θ_0 ~ N(0,1)` for *all* θ_0) holds exactly, then marginal PIT (`Φ(r_k(θ,X))` over the joint training distribution is `U(0,1)`) follows by integrating out θ. So diagnostic 2 is *implied* by diagnostic 3 in the population. They are not "independent tests" in any sample-level sense either — they share the same residuals, and a finite-sample test on the marginal is essentially a weighted average of the conditional KS statistics. The diagnostic *suite* is still useful because the marginal test has higher power per-sample (N = 5000–10000 marginal vs ~N/5 per conditional bin) and a different failure mode (Hermans's "marginal-pass conditional-fail" pattern), but the claim that they are independent measurements should be moderated.
(b) *Diagnostic 4 (joint Mahalanobis) is not implied by diagnostic 3 either.* In d > 1, having each `r_k|θ_0 ~ N(0,1)` does not imply `r|θ_0 ~ N(0,I_d)` (the components could be correlated). The manuscript correctly notes this in §8.2 ("the joint Mahalanobis test (which r_1, r_2 being individually calibrated does *not* automatically imply) passes"), but §7.3 does not state it explicitly when listing the diagnostics. Worth one sentence.
(c) *Sufficiency of the suite.* Coverage (diagnostic 5) is the inferential ground truth and *is* sufficient on its own for the practical question "does the constructed confidence set have its nominal coverage?" The other four are mechanistic checks that help diagnose *where* a failure lives. The §7.3 framing reads as if all five are jointly required; in practice the suite is best understood as "5 is the bar, 1–4 are diagnostics for why the bar is or is not met." The §8.4 ablation (where 5 fails and so does 3, but 2 nearly passes) illustrates this hierarchy nicely; §7.3 could foreshadow it.
**Suggested fix:** Add a one-line clarification at the end of §7.3 before the noise floor: "Diagnostic 5 is inferentially primary; diagnostics 1–4 are interpretability checks. Diagnostic 2 is implied by diagnostic 3 in the population; we report both because they have complementary finite-sample power. Diagnostic 4 is *not* implied by component-wise diagnostic 3 in `d > 1` (Hermans-type failure)."

### C-8.1-edge
**Verdict:** ⚠ under-justified
**Quote (lines 1410–1412):** "The conditional PIT shows degradation near the support boundary of `ρ` — a known finite-sample effect from undersampling near the support edges, not a structural issue."
**Diagnosis:** The "undersampling near edges" explanation is plausible but partly speculative as stated. With `θ ~ U[-7,7]` and KS evaluated at 7 θ_0 values, each conditional bin is *itself* of size ~N/7 ≈ 700 (giving noise floor `1.628/√700 ≈ 0.062`), so KS 0.05 at the edge is *within the per-bin noise floor* and need not invoke "support-edge undersampling" at all — it could simply be the smaller per-conditional sample size for the *fixed* θ_0. The "edge" claim is also slightly conflated: if conditional PIT is evaluated at *fixed* θ_0 with X ~ P_{θ_0}, the sample at that θ_0 *is* drawn from a single distribution and not affected by `ρ`'s edge undersampling — the edge effect would be relevant only if the *trained* model has poor extrapolation near the support boundary (which is the more likely real mechanism, but is a *structural* effect of finite training, not "finite-sample" in the testing sense). The distinction matters because the paper's framing throughout is that calibration is a property of the *trained* `r` on the deployment region.
**Suggested fix:** Replace with: "Conditional PIT degrades near `θ_0 = ±7` because the *trained* `r` is approximated more poorly there (the support boundary of the training proposal `ρ`), where the network sees fewer samples during training and the universal-approximation error is largest. The interior is uniformly clean. This is a *training-distribution* effect — see also the more careful discussion in §9 of choosing `ρ` to cover the deployment region with margin."

### C-8.3-interp
**Verdict:** ✗ error (dynamic-range claim is incorrect as stated)
**Quote (lines 1506–1511):** "The 'high' pivot RMSE of 0.27 is misleading: `r_2` has dynamic range ~12, so 0.27 is ~3% relative."
**Diagnosis:** This is the part the prompt flagged, and the concern is correct. Under the training proposal `θ ~ U[-7,7]^2` and `X|θ ~ N(θ,Σ)`, we have `θ − X ~ N(0,Σ)` *marginally over the proposal*, so `r_2 = −0.577(θ_1 − X_1) + 1.155(θ_2 − X_2)` has variance `0.577² + 1.155² − 2·0.577·1.155·0.5 ≈ 0.333 + 1.334 − 0.667 = 1.000`, i.e. `r_2` is marginally `N(0, 1)` over the training distribution. So the typical "spread" of `r_2` over training samples is ±3 (3-σ), giving an *effective range of about 6*, not 12. Under that scale, RMSE 0.27 is ~9% of the marginal-σ-based range, or ~4.5% of the half-range, not 3%. The author appears to have computed `r_2`'s range by plugging in extreme θ values *without realizing X|θ is concentrated around θ*, getting something like `|0.577·14| + |1.155·14| = 24` or similar — which over-counts because `θ − X` is *not* of order `θ`'s range; it's of order Σ^{1/2}. The recovered Jacobian + coverage diagnostics *do* still validate the experiment — the structural inference in the second half of the paragraph (that pointwise RMSE is the wrong diagnostic in multivariate, the right one is Jacobian + coverage) is correct and is the load-bearing point. Only the specific "dynamic range ~12, so 0.27 is ~3% relative" sentence is wrong on the numbers.
**Suggested fix:** Either (a) recompute: "`r_2` has standard deviation 1.0 under the training proposal, so 0.27 is ~27% of one σ — which would be a concerning pointwise mismatch *if* pointwise RMSE were the relevant diagnostic. It is not (see below); the relevant multivariate diagnostic is recovered Jacobian + coverage, both of which match the population truth to ~1–2%."; or (b) drop the dynamic-range argument entirely and lead with the Jacobian-matching argument, which is the genuine validation. Option (b) is cleaner because it removes a quantitatively-wrong defensive statement and puts the load-bearing claim (Theorem A-d / KR uniqueness validated by `E[J_θ] = L^{-1}`) in front.

### E-8.4
**Verdict:** ⚠ under-justified
**Quote (lines 1524–1568):** Setup `X_i ~ Exp(θ)` iid, `n = 5`, `T = ΣX_i`, `θ ~ U[0.3, 3.0]`, truth `r* = Φ^{−1}(F_{χ²_{10}}(2θT))`. Doubly-monotone architecture `r(θ,T) = b_umnn(T) + ∫_{θ_ref}^θ softplus(α(t) + β_umnn(T)) dt`. Results: pivot RMSE 0.045, final loss 0.87 vs truth 0.88, marginal PIT KS 0.006 (p=0.23), conditional PIT KS 0.016–0.028 (borderline vs N=6000 floor 0.021), coverage < 0.015.
**Diagnosis:** The conditional PIT range 0.016–0.028 straddles the noise floor 0.021. At conditional sample size N/5 = 1200, the per-bin noise floor is `1.628/√1200 ≈ 0.047`, so even the 0.028 endpoint is well *below* the per-bin floor — the experiment is cleanly passing the appropriate conditional test. The manuscript's "borderline (N=6000 noise floor ≈ 0.021)" comparison uses the *marginal* noise floor against a *conditional* statistic, which is the wrong comparison (a recurring issue — see C-8.2-borderline). The architecture itself is well-described: ∂_θ r is by construction softplus(·) > 0 (R1), ∂_T r is `b'_umnn(T) + β'_umnn(T) · ∫_{θ_ref}^θ σ(·) dt > 0` *provided* the integrand stays nonnegative — which it does because softplus is positive and σ ∈ (0,1) — so R2 holds in closed form. One nit: the formula at line 1540 writes `∂_T r = b'_umnn(T) + β'_umnn(T) ∫_{θ_ref}^θ σ(·) dt`, where `σ` is the derivative of softplus (the sigmoid), and the `∫_{θ_ref}^θ σ(·) dt` term *can* be negative when `θ < θ_ref` (the lower limit is greater than the upper) — but since both `b'_umnn` and `β'_umnn` are positive and the second term then enters with a negative integral, monotonicity depends on `b'_umnn(T) > |β'_umnn(T) · ∫|` in that regime. This is fine in practice (the dependence on the lower-limit choice is absorbed into bias(c)) but the "in closed form" claim of strict positivity for *all* θ < θ_ref is not immediate from the formula.
**Suggested fix:** (a) Replace the marginal noise-floor comparison with the per-bin one (or just remove "borderline" — at the appropriate per-bin floor of ~0.047, the values are clean). (b) Add half a sentence clarifying that `θ_ref` is typically taken to be the lower endpoint of the support of `θ` (so the integral is always over `[θ_ref, θ]` with `θ ≥ θ_ref`), making the monotonicity manifest.

### C-8.4-ablation-interp
**Verdict:** ✓ holds
**Quote (lines 1576–1586):** "[...] when `T ↦ r` is non-bijective, `p̂_r` is not normalized in T at fixed θ, so `−log p̂_r` has no lower bound at the conditional entropy. Concretely, in regions where the autograd `|∂r/∂T|` is locally inflated by a fold, `log|∂r/∂T|` contributes a large positive value to the surrogate log-density without `p̂_r` being a valid density."
**Diagnosis:** The logical argument is correct. Decomposing as in §3.2 (line 460–462): for a *valid* `p̂_r` (bijective in X at fixed θ), `L(r) = E_ρ[KL(p ‖ p̂_r)] + const`, where `const` is the conditional entropy `H(X|θ)` averaged over ρ. The truth `r*` achieves KL = 0, hence loss = conditional entropy = 0.88. By Gibbs's inequality, KL ≥ 0, so any valid `p̂_r` has loss ≥ 0.88. The ablation's loss = 0.56 < 0.88 therefore proves the ablation's `p̂_r` is *not* a valid density — the only way a *strictly-proper* objective produces a value below the entropy bound is if the objective is being computed on an object that isn't a density. The mechanistic explanation ("autograd `|∂r/∂T|` inflated by a fold") is the right local description of how this occurs in optimization. The conditional-entropy interpretation of the 0.88 number is exactly what §3.2 establishes; the ablation's 0.56 < 0.88 directly demonstrates C-3.5-loss-below. This is one of the strongest empirical anchors in the paper.
**Suggested fix:** None. (One stylistic suggestion: explicitly point back to the bound — "By §3.2, the conditional-entropy lower bound on the NF-MLE loss is 0.88 in this model; the ablation produces 0.56, falsifying the implicit claim that its `p̂_r` is a valid density.")

### C-8.5-recipe
**Verdict:** ⚠ under-justified
**Quote (lines 1658–1672):** "The architectural prescription distilled across all four cases: 1. NF-MLE loss; 2. Monotone in θ, architecturally (R1) — UMNN integration in θ; 3. Monotone in X, architecturally (R2) — additive structure with monotone-in-X heads, triangular flow in d > 1, or doubly-monotone UMNN for non-additive X-dependence."
**Diagnosis:** The recipe is a fair *synthesis* of what was tested, but framing it as "the architectural prescription distilled across all four cases" overstates the empirical support. The four experiments cover: 1D additive (§8.1), 2D additive uncorrelated (§8.2), 2D additive correlated (§8.3), and 1D non-additive via exponential rate using sufficient statistic T (§8.4). All four have the truth `r*` *in or near* the additive class (§8.1–8.3) or in a *single*-doubly-monotone class with a one-dimensional sufficient statistic (§8.4). Untested by §8: (a) genuinely multivariate non-additive cases where the doubly-monotone construction must be applied per-coordinate in a triangular fashion (the d > 1 generalization of §8.4); (b) cases where `X` is high-dimensional without an obvious low-dim sufficient statistic (a key SBI use case); (c) cases where the "right" architectural choice from §9's decision table is *not* obvious in advance. The recipe is *necessary* (the §8.4 ablation shows what happens when R2 is dropped) and *sufficient for the cases tested*, but the §8 evidence does not span the full claim. The recipe also doesn't separate "NF-MLE is necessary" (it isn't — alternative strictly-proper losses exist per §3.7) from "NF-MLE is what we recommend" — the experiments only test NF-MLE, so they don't *empirically* rule out other Class-5 or Class-2 losses; the rule-out is theoretical (§3.7).
**Suggested fix:** Add a calibrating sentence: "The recipe is *sufficient* for the architectures and models tested in §8.1–8.4; broader empirical validation (high-d X, fully non-additive multivariate, fewer-architectural-choices regimes) is open work (§11). Among strictly-proper alternatives (§3.7), NF-MLE is recommended on the architectural-compatibility and finite-sample grounds analyzed in §3.7, not because §8 empirically rules out other losses."
