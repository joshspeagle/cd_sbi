## Per-problem findings

### OP-11.1 (Uniqueness beyond the autoregressive triangular class)
**Verdict:** ⚠ under-justified / mischaracterized
**Quote (lines 1813–1822):** "the *full* calibration manifold over more flexible architectures (e.g., dense flows, non-autoregressive coupling layers) is generically not a singleton --- orthogonal rotations of `r` preserve calibration since `N(0, I_d)` is rotation-invariant. Whether NF-MLE with looser architectural constraints still converges to a canonical pivot, or whether the practitioner must commit to a specific autoregressive structure to guarantee uniqueness, is open."
**Diagnosis:** Two issues. (i) The statement "orthogonal rotations of `r` preserve calibration" is true for the calibration constraint alone (C1: distribution `N(0, I_d)`), but the calibration manifold M as defined in §2.2/§3.3 also requires (R1) and (R2). (R1) is *strict autoregressive monotonicity in θ-coordinates*: for an arbitrary rotation `Q ∈ O(d)`, the map `θ_k ↦ (Q r)_k` is generically *not* monotone in θ_k at fixed θ_{<k}, since rows of Q mix coordinates. So the rotation symmetry is not preserved by (R1) in the autoregressive sense — only by coordinate-wise sign-flip subgroups (the Z₂^d already noted in §2.3) and by rotations that happen to act block-triangularly. The sentence as written conflates "in the calibration-only class M_F (no (R1), (R2))" with "in the loose-architecture class". (ii) The §3.3 forward statement is more careful — "in multivariate, rotations of a calibrated pivot stay calibrated" — but §11.1 elides whether (R1)/(R2) restrict to a smaller subgroup. The relevant open question is therefore: which subgroup of `O(d)` acts on M for a *non-autoregressive* architecture that still enforces some weaker form of (R1)/(R2)? That's a genuine open question, but it is *not* "all rotations."

The "is open" claim itself stands: uniqueness within general non-autoregressive architectures (continuous normalizing flows, RealNVP-style coupling, etc.) is genuinely not resolved in the CD-SBI literature.
**Suggested fix:** Replace the rotation-invariance one-liner with: "Orthogonal rotations of `r` preserve the calibration constraint `r ~ N(0, I_d)`; under (R1), only a subgroup of `O(d)` preserves the monotonicity structure (e.g. coordinate-wise sign flips). Whether residual ambiguities exist beyond this — and how (R1)/(R2) restrict the symmetry group of M for non-triangular architectures — is open." Also forward-cite §3.3 explicitly to make consistent.

### OP-11.2 (Ordering selection in KR)
**Verdict:** ⚠ under-justified
**Quote (lines 1825–1835):** "Whether some orderings have better finite-sample optimization behavior --- easier-to-learn conditionals, lower variance gradients --- is an empirical question worth investigating. The optimal-transport literature (Carlier–Galichon–Santambrogio 2010) connects KR maps with `t → 1` to Brenier maps, suggesting that a more isotropic transport target might give better-behaved finite-sample training; this is unexplored in the CD-SBI context."
**Diagnosis:** Two points. (i) The Carlier–Galichon–Santambrogio (2010) connection is correctly described — they prove the continuous interpolation from KR to Brenier maps via Knothe-type rearrangements parametrised by an entropic/diagonal-scaling factor going to zero. The claim that "more isotropic transport target might give better-behaved finite-sample training" is genuinely speculative; this is fine in an open-problems section but the framing should make clearer that it is conjecture, not literature-supported. Since 2010 there has been substantial activity on entropic-OT-style smoothings (Cuturi 2013, Genevay et al. 2018, Feydy et al. 2019) and on triangular-flow training stability (Jaini et al. 2019 SOS flows, Wehenkel & Louppe 2019 UMNN, Huang et al. 2018 NAF), but to my knowledge no paper has directly studied "which KR ordering trains best" empirically in the SBI / normalizing-flow context. (ii) The claim is therefore genuinely open, but the framing risks suggesting the OT literature provides guidance. It does not directly — KR-to-Brenier interpolation is a structural result about the map, not a statement about finite-sample training noise of a learned triangular flow.
**Suggested fix:** Add one sentence: "We are not aware of any published study comparing KR orderings on finite-sample training stability in either SBI or general normalizing-flow contexts." Drop "suggesting" and reword as "raises the conjecture that". Keep the open-problem status.

### OP-11.3 (The C⁰ residual in 1D: (R3) from (R1) + Lipschitz + calibration?)
**Verdict:** ⚠ under-justified (the framing is misleading about *why* the question is open)
**Quote (lines 1838–1842):** "Whether (R3) follows from (R1) + Lipschitz + calibration alone is open. Lemma 4.3 gives first-order rigidity, so any failure mode is isolated; in practice, architectural enforcement makes the question moot."
**Diagnosis:** Three issues. (i) Lemma 4.3 (the strict-propriety lemma) does *not* "give first-order rigidity" — it only says any minimizer satisfies `r(θ₀;X) | θ₀ ~ N(0,1)`. The first-order / Taylor-expansion machinery that gives "failure mode is isolated" actually lives in Lemma 4.2 (level-set rigidity from `C^1` + calibration → strict monotonicity), which is exactly what is *unavailable* in the Lipschitz setting: that's the whole point of needing (R3) as a separate assumption (see §4.4, lines 802–805). The citation here is wrong. (ii) The actual obstruction is the failure of the pushforward-density divergence argument when `ψ'` is allowed to be zero on a positive-measure set (Lipschitz V-shapes / sawtooths) — the contradiction in the proof of Lemma 4.2 is the `|v − v*|^{-1/2}` blow-up of the pushforward density at a critical value, which fails for ReLU-style functions. (iii) Note that in pure 1D measure-theoretic terms, the strictly monotone increasing measure-preserving map between two atomless probability measures on ℝ is unique (this is classical), so the question is really: does (R1) + Lipschitz + calibration force `X ↦ r(θ₀;X)` to be *strictly* monotone (not merely monotone non-decreasing)? The standard counterexample template — Lipschitz `ψ` with a flat interval — fails calibration (it would push positive mass to a single point), so the question is whether *non-strict* but a.e.-strict monotonicity can occur. That is a genuinely subtle measure-theoretic question, and I don't believe it is resolved by standard monotone-rearrangement results (which assume strict monotonicity).

The question is genuinely open; the framing just misattributes the relevant lemma and underspecifies the obstruction.
**Suggested fix:** Replace "Lemma 4.3 gives first-order rigidity, so any failure mode is isolated" with: "Lemma 4.2 (level-set rigidity) gives the result in the `C^1` case; its proof relies on a pushforward-density blow-up that fails for Lipschitz functions with critical sets of positive measure. Whether a measure-theoretic substitute exists — e.g., that any non-strictly-monotone Lipschitz `r` calibrated under (R1) must have a.e. constant sign of `∂_X r` — is the precise open question." Keep the in-practice-moot sentence.

### OP-11.4 (Misspecification)
**Verdict:** ⚠ under-justified (terminology and recent-literature gap)
**Quote (lines 1849–1853):** "the population minimizer of NF-MLE is no longer the true CD pivot but rather the moment-projection of the model density onto the architectural class. What this object's coverage properties are --- and whether CD-SBI inherits a degraded but still useful guarantee --- is an open question."
**Diagnosis:** The term "moment-projection" is the wrong characterization. NF-MLE is the ρ-weighted expected forward-KL minimizer: `argmin_F E_ρ[KL(p(·|θ) ‖ p̂_r(·|θ))]`. The argument of the inner KL has the *model* `p̂_r` second, so this is the *M-projection* (information / moment projection in Csiszár's terminology when the family is in the second slot is genuinely the M-projection — confusingly, the modern ML literature increasingly calls this the "moment projection" because it matches moments of the data distribution; classical Bayesian / variational papers (Minka 2005) call the *other* direction the M-projection). The terminology has split: in the modern Bregman/exponential-family geometric sense, `argmin KL(p ‖ q)` over `q ∈ F` is the "moment matching" or "M-projection" (forward-KL), so the text is consistent with one common convention but inconsistent with another (Minka). More importantly the statement "moment-projection of the *model density* onto the architectural class" is potentially misleading — it should say "of the *true conditional density* onto the architectural class," since `p(·|θ)` is the true model, `p̂_r` the parametric flow. (ii) Wehenkel et al. (2025), cited in the same paragraph, do study coverage degradation under misspecification — specifically a robust SBI framework with prior-predictive checks and a generalized Bayes objective. To my knowledge they do *not* prove a clean coverage-degradation bound for NF-MLE in the forward-KL-projection sense; their robustness mechanism is reweighting (RKL-style), not a coverage analysis of the M-projection. So the open-problem status is correct, but the framing should acknowledge that Wehenkel et al. address a *related* but not identical question.
**Suggested fix:** Replace "moment-projection of the model density" with "forward-KL (or 'M-')projection of the true conditional `p(·|θ)` onto the architectural class". Add: "Wehenkel et al. (2025) study robustness via a reweighted/generalized Bayes objective; a direct coverage-degradation analysis of the forward-KL projection of `p` onto F is, to our knowledge, still missing." Both keep "open" status while making the obstruction precise.

### OP-11.5 (Higher-dimensional scaling)
**Verdict:** ⚠ under-justified (the d≳10 cutoff is unsupported speculation)
**Quote (lines 1856–1861):** "For `d ≳ 10`, careful architecture design (sparse autoregressive structure, attention-based conditioning) likely becomes important. Empirical validation up to `d = 10` is a natural next milestone."
**Diagnosis:** The d ≳ 10 cutoff is not justified by any citation or argument. Triangular / autoregressive flows have been deployed at substantially higher dimensions in the broader normalizing-flow literature (Kingma & Dhariwal 2018 Glow at d ~ thousands for images; Durkan et al. 2019 neural spline flows at d ~ 100; Papamakarios et al. 2017 MAF at d ~ 80). In the SBI literature specifically, SNL/SNPE-C have been applied with autoregressive flows up to d ~ 30 (e.g. Lueckmann et al. 2021 benchmark, Hermans et al. 2022). So while CD-SBI's *specific* construction has not yet been pushed past 2D in the experiments, the "d ≳ 10 needs special design" claim is genuinely speculative and inconsistent with adjacent literature. The deeper open question — whether the *calibration-monotonicity-Jacobian* triple of architectural constraints can be maintained efficiently at high d — is real and well-posed; the d=10 cutoff is not.
**Suggested fix:** Replace "For `d ≳ 10`, careful architecture design (...) likely becomes important" with: "At higher d (extant SBI benchmarks reach d ~ 30 with standard autoregressive flows; image-scale d requires sparse/coupling architectures), maintaining the calibration-monotonicity-Jacobian triple jointly may require specialized design. Empirical validation past d=2 is a natural next milestone." This grounds the claim and avoids an unjustified threshold.

### OP-11.6 (Sequential / amortized variants)
**Verdict:** ⚠ under-justified (the obstruction is correctly named but the literature should be acknowledged)
**Quote (lines 1864–1871):** "Whether the calibration guarantee survives sequential refinement is non-trivial: the implicit conditioning on the iterative procedure changes the population-level distribution that the loss converges to."
**Diagnosis:** The framing is correct on the obstruction: sequential SBI (SNPE-A/B/C, SNL with sequential simulation budget) changes the population law by adapting ρ to concentrate near X_obs, which breaks the "ρ-independence of the population pivot" argument (cf. §1.4, §9). However: (i) Lueckmann et al. 2021 (the benchmark paper) and Hermans et al. 2022 have documented that *frequentist coverage* is empirically poor for all sequential SBI variants — the calibration guarantee does indeed fail empirically when ρ is iteratively refined. So the answer to "does frequentist coverage survive sequential refinement" is almost certainly *no* in any naive implementation, and this is essentially known. (ii) Restated as "whether a *modified* sequential procedure (e.g., with importance reweighting, like APT / SNPE-C does for the posterior) preserves the calibration guarantee," it is genuinely open — that is the right open question. (iii) Falkiewicz et al. 2023 (cited in §10 for Calibrated NPE) is the closest existing work; they add a differentiable coverage regularizer to the sequential loop but do not prove a calibration guarantee.

The text is therefore mixing a near-resolved question ("does naive sequential preserve calibration?" — almost certainly no) with a genuinely open one ("can a corrected sequential procedure preserve it?").
**Suggested fix:** Split into two sub-questions: "(a) Naive sequential CD-SBI almost certainly loses pointwise calibration — adaptive ρ violates the ρ-independence argument of §1.4. (b) Whether an importance-reweighted or APT-style correction restores calibration is open and is the natural problem." Cite Lueckmann et al. 2021 and Hermans et al. 2022 for (a) and Falkiewicz et al. 2023 as related work for (b).

### OP-11.7 (Discrete data with no scalar sufficient statistic)
**Verdict:** ✓ open & accurate
**Quote (lines 1875–1878):** "the discrete extension of §5.7 must be combined with feature extraction. Whether the Lancaster randomization argument extends to learned-feature settings is open."
**Diagnosis:** This is correctly characterized as open. The randomization argument in §5.7 relies on `T(X)` being a fixed (scalar, MLR) sufficient statistic; (R4) (order-preservation in T) is the load-bearing assumption that selects `V*_θ` from the family of calibrated alternatives. If `T(X) = g_φ(X)` is a *learned* feature, then (i) `g_φ` is not sufficient in general (so the canonical V*_θ doesn't exist exactly), (ii) (R4) is no longer architecturally clean since the ordering of T-values is learned alongside the pivot, and (iii) randomization across atoms requires knowing the atom probabilities `p_θ(t)` per learned feature — a feature-extractor-dependent quantity. No work I'm aware of (in either the CD literature or in SBI for discrete data) has resolved this; Greenberg et al. 2019 (SNPE-C) handle discrete data but for the posterior not the CD; Cole et al. 2022 / Miller et al. 2022 (NRE on discrete data) sidestep the issue via likelihood ratios.

The "is open" framing is right; the obstruction is correctly identified (Lancaster randomization needs a fixed sufficient statistic).
**Suggested fix:** None essential; could optionally note that the obstruction is specifically the loss of (R4)-style order coherence under a learned feature map.

### OP-11.8 (Efficient C_α computation in d ≥ 3)
**Verdict:** ✗ not actually open (engineering / well-understood)
**Quote (lines 1882–1887):** "Inversion via `‖r(θ, X_obs)‖² ≤ χ²_{d,α}` requires evaluating `r` on a grid or via root-finding. For visualization and reporting, efficient confidence-set computation in `d ≥ 3` --- perhaps via direct sampling from the implicit set, or via a learned set-boundary parameterization --- is worth developing."
**Diagnosis:** This is an engineering / implementation question, not an open theoretical or methodological problem. The level set `{θ : ‖r(θ,X_obs)‖² ≤ χ²_{d,α}}` is the image of a ball under the inverse of a known monotone-autoregressive map; sampling from it is a standard problem solvable by (i) inverting `r(·;X_obs)` autoregressively (§6.4 already says "1D root-finding per coordinate" — this directly gives boundary sampling), (ii) HMC / SMC / rejection sampling on the implicit set, or (iii) the standard pushforward: sample `z ~ N(0,I_d)` constrained to the χ² ball, then invert `r(·; X_obs)(θ) = z`. None of these are research-grade open problems; they are method-implementation choices already routine in conformal/likelihood-ratio confidence-set literatures (e.g. Lei et al. 2018 conformal sets, Dalmasso et al. 2024 LF2I). The "learned set-boundary parameterization" framing is a plausible research direction but is not a *necessary* open problem — the standard approaches already work.
**Suggested fix:** Either (a) remove from the open-problems list and move to §9 (Implementation recipe) as a practical note on confidence-set visualization, or (b) reframe as: "While `C_α` is computationally well-defined via autoregressive 1D root-finding (§6.4), efficient *visualization and reporting* in d ≥ 3 (especially when only boundary samples or projections are needed) is an engineering question worth developing." This downgrades from "open problem" to "implementation challenge."

## Cross-Part findings
- [Part I, R1 / §2.3]: The §11.1 rotation-invariance discussion is in tension with the §2.3 statement that (R1) eliminates the `Z₂^d` (coordinate sign-flip) subgroup. If the full rotation symmetry of `N(0,I_d)` is invoked in §11.1 as an "open" source of non-uniqueness, the framing should specify that (R1) already kills a discrete subgroup; the remaining continuous ambiguity is over `O(d) / Z₂^d` modulo (R1)-compatible block-triangular rotations. This is also relevant to C-1.3-C2 (which already flagged the autoregressive-vs-coordinate-wise monotonicity ambiguity).
- [Part II, §3.3 line 491–494]: The phrase "in 1D, any composition of a monotone transport... with a measure-preserving bijection of `N(0,1)` to itself" is technically incorrect — the only orientation-preserving measure-preserving bijection of `N(0,1)` to itself (in the strict-monotonicity sense) is the identity (by 1D monotone rearrangement uniqueness, Lemma 4.1). The "enormous calibration manifold" claim therefore really comes from dropping monotonicity, not from "measure-preserving bijections of N(0,1)". This is relevant to OP-11.1 (which inherits the same loose framing) and to OP-11.3.
- [Part III, §4.4 / Lemma 4.2–4.3]: OP-11.3 cites Lemma 4.3 for "first-order rigidity," but the relevant lemma is Lemma 4.2 (level-set rigidity via pushforward-density blow-up). Suggest correcting the cross-reference.
- [Part V/VI, §8 and §10]: OP-11.5 (d ≳ 10 cutoff) and OP-11.6 (sequential variants) both ignore directly relevant prior empirical work. OP-11.5 should cite the standard NF benchmarks (Papamakarios 2017, Durkan 2019) and SBI benchmark (Lueckmann 2021) showing autoregressive flows scale past d=10 routinely; OP-11.6 should cite Hermans et al. 2022 (overconfidence of sequential SBI — same paper cited in C-1.1-overconf) and Falkiewicz et al. 2023 (Calibrated NPE) as directly relevant.
