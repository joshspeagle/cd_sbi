## Per-claim findings

### D-autoflow
**Verdict:** ⚠ under-justified
**Quote (from .tex, lines 1053–1061):** "(R1^auto) Autoregressive monotonicity in θ: r_k depends on θ only through θ_{≤k}, and θ_k ↦ r_k is strictly increasing at fixed θ_{<k}, X. (R2^auto) Autoregressive monotonicity in X: r_k depends on X only through X_{≤k}, and X_k ↦ r_k is strictly monotone at fixed θ, X_{<k}."
**Diagnosis:** The "depends only on X_{≤k}" clause in (R2^auto) is *essential*, not optional. It is what makes ∂_X r lower-triangular (C-6.2-tri-jac) and what makes σ(θ, r_{<k}) = σ(θ, X_{<k}) in the inductive step of T-A-d. Without the dependence restriction, the induction could not pass from "r_{<k} determined by (θ, X_{<k})" to "the (θ, X_{<k})-conditional law of r_k equals the (θ, r_{<k})-conditional law", and the Jacobian would not be triangular. (R2^auto) as currently stated combines two distinct requirements (dependence-restriction + monotonicity-in-X_k); the manuscript should flag that both are load-bearing. Note also a notational inconsistency: (R1^auto) says "at fixed θ_{<k}, X" (full X), but since r_k depends on X only through X_{≤k}, this is equivalent to "at fixed θ_{<k}, X_{≤k}" — minor, but the asymmetry vs. (R2^auto)'s "fixed θ, X_{<k}" is jarring.
**Suggested fix:** Split (R2^auto) into (R2a^auto) dependence — "r_k is a measurable function of (θ_{≤k}, X_{≤k}) alone" — and (R2b^auto) monotonicity — "for fixed θ, X_{<k}, the map X_k ↦ r_k is strictly monotone (with consistent sign)". Then call out in §6.3 that (R2a^auto) is what aligns σ-algebras in the induction.

### D-additive
**Verdict:** ✓ holds
**Quote (line 1070):** "r_k(θ, X) = a_k(θ_k; θ_<k, X_<k) − b_k(X_k; θ_<k, X_<k), with a_k a UMNN monotone in θ_k and b_k a UMNN monotone in X_k."
**Diagnosis:** Correct as a definition. The "monotone in θ_k" for a_k and "monotone in X_k" for b_k (with appropriate signs) give (R1^auto) and (R2^auto). The conditioning of a_k on X_<k is what allows non-trivial off-diagonal coupling in ∂_X r.

### D-doublymono
**Verdict:** ⚠ under-justified
**Quote (line 1075):** "r_k(θ, X) = b_k(X_k; X_<k) + ∫_{θ_{k,ref}}^{θ_k} softplus(α_k(t; θ_<k, X_<k) + β_k(X_k; X_<k)) dt"
**Diagnosis:** Two issues. (i) The β_k(X_k; X_<k) inside the integrand makes ∂_{θ_k} r_k = softplus(...) > 0 (giving R1^auto monotonicity in θ_k), but ∂_{X_k} r_k = ∂_{X_k} b_k + ∫ softplus'(·) · ∂_{X_k} β_k dt. The first term is positive (b_k a UMNN); the second is *not necessarily positive* — softplus' ∈ (0,1) and ∂_{X_k} β_k can be of either sign. (R2^auto) requires monotonicity in X_k, which is only guaranteed if ∂_{X_k} β_k ≥ 0 — i.e., β_k is itself a UMNN in X_k. (ii) The statement "monotone in X_k by appropriate UMNN sub-structure on b_k and β_k" punts on this. The definition would be more honest if it explicitly required β_k to be monotone non-decreasing in X_k.
**Suggested fix:** Add the explicit requirement "β_k is a UMNN (or constant) in X_k at fixed X_<k" so that ∂_{X_k} r_k = ∂_{X_k} b_k + ∫ softplus' · ∂_{X_k} β_k dt is a sum of non-negative terms with at least one strictly positive (the b_k term).

### C-6.2-tri-jac
**Verdict:** ⚠ under-justified
**Quote (lines 1086–1094):** "[∂_X r]_{k,ℓ} = sgn · ∂_{X_k} r_k for k=ℓ, (free) for ℓ<k, 0 for ℓ>k ... log|det ∂_X r| = Σ_k log|∂_{X_k} r_k|."
**Diagnosis:** Lower-triangularity of ∂_X r is correct: r_k depends on X only through X_{≤k}, so ∂_{X_ℓ} r_k = 0 for ℓ > k. The diagonal entries ∂_{X_k} r_k are nonzero by (R2^auto) strict monotonicity. The determinant identity follows. However: (a) the "sgn" notation in the diagonal is unexplained — what is the sign convention, and is it the same across coordinates? (R2^auto) only says monotone, not necessarily increasing. If b_k is monotone *decreasing* in X_k (additive form), then ∂_{X_k} r_k = −∂_{X_k} b_k could be positive or negative depending on b_k's direction. For the calibration manifold to be the singleton KR map, the sign must be consistent (matching r_k^KR = Φ^{-1}(1 − F_k), which is monotone *decreasing* in X_k). The manuscript hints at this with "(R1^auto) fixes the sign" in the proof of T-A-d, but should also pin down the sign of ∂_X r for the Jacobian formula. (b) "All closed-form from the UMNN positive integrand" — true for the additive form (∂_X r_k = −∂_X b_k = −softplus(MLP(X_k, ·)) is closed form), and for the doubly-monotone form's b_k term, but the doubly-monotone form's contribution ∫ softplus' · ∂_{X_k} β_k dt is *not* closed-form — it requires numerical integration in θ. This should be acknowledged.
**Suggested fix:** State the sign convention explicitly (e.g., ∂_{X_k} r_k < 0, matching the KR map). For the doubly-monotone form, clarify that the X-Jacobian's `b_k` piece is closed-form but the `β_k`-via-integral piece needs numerical integration if β_k actually depends on X_k.

### C-6.2-Olin
**Verdict:** ✓ holds
**Quote (line 1103):** "All operations cost O(d) per sample, vs. O(d³) for a general dense Jacobian."
**Diagnosis:** Correct, modulo conditioning network costs (each r_k carries a conditioning MLP taking θ_<k, X_<k as input; that MLP's forward pass is O(k) input dim, so total is O(d²) in conditioning-MLP input dim if the MLP width scales with d). ### C-6.2-connectedCI
**Verdict:** ⚠ under-justified
**Quote (lines 1098–1101):** "r is monotone-increasing in θ_k at fixed θ_{<k}, so H_r(·; X) has connected level sets in the natural autoregressive sense. Confidence sets via the chi-square inversion are well-defined."
**Diagnosis:** The "connected level sets in the natural autoregressive sense" is left informal. What is meant is: for fixed X, the sub-level set {θ : ‖r(θ; X)‖² ≤ c} can be parameterized autoregressively — fix θ_<k, then the θ_k-slice is an interval by monotonicity of θ_k ↦ r_k. But the *full* sub-level set being connected as a subset of ℝ^d requires more than pointwise autoregressive monotonicity: it requires that the autoregressive slice θ_k-intervals vary continuously in θ_<k, which is true under C¹ but should be argued. Compare to standard univariate connectedness (under R1, the sub-level set of (θ−X)² is an interval — trivial). In multidimensional autoregressive case, connectedness is not automatic from pointwise slice intervals — it requires continuity of the slice endpoints. With C¹ structure this holds, but the manuscript should not present it as a one-line corollary.
**Suggested fix:** State explicitly: "Under (R1^auto) + C¹, the sub-level set {θ : ‖r(θ; X)‖² ≤ c} is the image of a continuous autoregressive interval-valued selection, hence connected." Or, alternatively, weaken the claim to "the autoregressive root-finding in §6.4 yields a well-defined slice-by-slice characterization" and drop the connectedness claim, which isn't really used elsewhere.

### T-A-d
**Verdict:** ⚠ under-justified
**Quote (lines 1148–1163):** "Inductive step (k > 1). Assume r_1,...,r_{k-1} are uniquely identified. ... since r_1,...,r_{k-1} are deterministic functions of (θ, X_<k), conditioning on them (given θ) is equivalent to conditioning on X_<k. Hence r_k(θ, X_≤k) | θ, X_<k ~ N(0,1). By (R2^auto), as a function of X_k this map is monotone; by Theorem A applied in X_k-coordinates with conditional law F_k^{(θ)}(· | X_<k) playing the role of the Gaussian source, it is uniquely Φ^{-1}(1 − F_k^{(θ)}(X_k | X_<k)). (R1^auto) fixes the sign of the dependence on θ_k. ∎"
**Diagnosis:** Three gaps. (1) "conditioning on r_<k is equivalent to conditioning on X_<k": this requires σ(θ, r_<k) = σ(θ, X_<k). One direction (σ(r_<k) ⊆ σ(X_<k)) is the deterministic-function statement; the other (σ(X_<k) ⊆ σ(r_<k)) needs the map X_<k ↦ r_<k to be *invertible* given θ. This holds by (R2^auto)'s strict monotonicity at each previous level — but the manuscript never says this. The induction therefore implicitly uses (R2^auto) *at every previous level*, not just at level k. (2) "by Theorem A applied in X_k-coordinates with conditional law F_k^{(θ)}(·|X_<k) playing the role of the Gaussian source": Theorem A is stated for X ~ N(θ, 1). Its proof uses Lemmas 4.1 and 4.2. Lemma 4.1 (monotone transport between atomless laws) generalizes immediately. Lemma 4.2 (C¹ rigidity) uses Gaussian-density positivity (line 752: φ(X_i − θ_0) → φ(X_* − θ_0) > 0) and finiteness of target density. The argument carries over verbatim provided the source conditional density is positive on its support and the target (still N(0,1)) is finite — both true under the hypothesis "F_k^{(θ)}(·|X_<k) is C¹ and strictly increasing on its support". But the manuscript glosses this: it should say "Lemmas 4.1–4.2 use only atomlessness and positivity of the source density on its support — both satisfied by F_k^{(θ)}(·|X_<k) under the stated hypothesis — so the argument transports." (3) The induction is over k = 1, 2, ..., d, but the inductive hypothesis is that "r_1, ..., r_{k-1} are uniquely identified", which is the *conclusion* of the previous step. This needs the strong claim "r_j = Φ^{-1}(1 − F_j^{(θ)}(X_j | X_{<j}))" at all j < k, which is fine modulo (1) and (2).
**Suggested fix:** Add a sentence: "By (R2^auto) at each level j < k, the map X_j ↦ r_j is a strictly monotone bijection at fixed (θ, X_{<j}); composing autoregressively, (θ, X_<k) ↦ (θ, r_<k) is a triangular bijection, so σ(θ, X_<k) = σ(θ, r_<k)." And: "Lemmas 4.1 and 4.2 (proof of Theorem A) use only atomlessness of the source and positivity of its density on the support; both hold for F_k^{(θ)}(·|X_<k) under the stated hypothesis, so the conclusion of Theorem A transports to this conditional setting."

### C-6.3-Gaussian
**Verdict:** ⚠ under-justified
**Quote (lines 1173–1188):** "The conditional X_k | θ, X_<k is Gaussian with mean μ_k = θ_k + Σ_{j<k} α_{kj}(X_j − θ_j) and variance σ_k² = L_{kk}², where the α_{kj}, σ_k² are the Cholesky regression coefficients. Direct computation: F_k^{(θ)}(X_k | X_<k) = Φ((X_k − μ_k)/σ_k), so r_k^KR = Φ^{-1}(1 − F_k) = (μ_k − X_k)/σ_k = ... = [L^{-1}(θ − X)]_k."
**Diagnosis:** The endpoint is correct, but the algebra is glossed in a way that obscures a real subtlety. Setting Z = L^{-1}(X − θ), we have Z ~ N(0, I_d), so X = θ + LZ, and Z_j is σ(X_{≤j})-measurable. Thus E[X_k | θ, X_<k] = θ_k + Σ_{j<k} L_{kj} Z_j, where Z_j is the j-th Cholesky residual. The manuscript writes μ_k as a linear combination of (X_j − θ_j), but **the coefficients are NOT L_{kj}**. Solving: from L^{-1}(X − θ) = Z, the k-th row gives Σ_{j≤k} (L^{-1})_{kj} (X_j − θ_j) = Z_k, so X_k − θ_k = L_{kk} Z_k − L_{kk} Σ_{j<k} (L^{-1})_{kj} (X_j − θ_j). Hence α_{kj} = −L_{kk} (L^{-1})_{kj}, not L_{kj}. The final identity r_k = (μ_k − X_k)/L_{kk} = [L^{-1}(θ − X)]_k is correct, and I verified the algebra:
  r_k = [θ_k − L_{kk}Σ_{j<k}(L^{-1})_{kj}(X_j − θ_j) − X_k]/L_{kk} = (1/L_{kk})(θ_k − X_k) + Σ_{j<k}(L^{-1})_{kj}(θ_j − X_j) = Σ_{j≤k}(L^{-1})_{kj}(θ_j − X_j) = [L^{-1}(θ−X)]_k. ✓
But this requires knowing that α_{kj} = −L_{kk}(L^{-1})_{kj}; the manuscript's "= [L^{-1}(θ−X)]_k" step is essentially asserted, not derived. The 2D numerical check is correct: L = [[1,0],[0.5, √0.75]], L^{-1} ≈ [[1,0],[−0.5774, 1.1547]], r_2 = 1.1547(θ_2 − X_2) − 0.5774(θ_1 − X_1). ✓
**Suggested fix:** Either (a) explicitly identify α_{kj} = −L_{kk}(L^{-1})_{kj} as the Cholesky regression coefficient (this is a standard but easily-confused identity — see e.g. Pourahmadi 1999 "Joint mean-covariance models" or any modern multivariate-Gaussian-via-Cholesky reference), or (b) do the derivation more directly: start from L^{-1}(X − θ) ~ N(0, I_d) (by definition of Cholesky), so −L^{-1}(X − θ) = L^{-1}(θ − X) ~ N(0, I_d), and this is autoregressively-triangular in X (since L^{-1} is lower-triangular) and monotone-increasing in θ (since diagonal of L^{-1} is positive), and uniqueness from T-A-d closes the argument. Option (b) bypasses the regression-coefficient framing entirely and is cleaner.

### C-6.3-ordering
**Verdict:** ⚠ under-justified
**Quote (lines 1192–1200):** "Different orderings of the coordinates of X (and correspondingly of θ at each autoregressive level) yield different KR pivots, all valid CD pivots, all in their respective calibration manifolds."
**Diagnosis:** True under the implicit assumption that for *every* permutation π, the conditionals F_{π(k)}^{(θ)}(· | X_{π(<k)}) satisfy the regularity hypothesis of T-A-d (C¹ and strictly increasing on support). For generic continuous models this is fine, but it is not free — e.g., for a model where the joint density has lower regularity in some directions than others, certain orderings may produce conditionals that fail the C¹ hypothesis. The claim should be stated conditionally: "Under the regularity hypothesis of T-A-d applied to the permuted ordering..." For Gaussian models (the only worked example), the hypothesis holds for every ordering, so the corollary in §8.3 is unaffected.
**Suggested fix:** Add a parenthetical: "(assuming the conditional CDFs under the chosen permutation also satisfy the C¹-strictly-increasing hypothesis of Theorem A-d)". This is automatic for full-support continuous densities like multivariate Gaussian.

### C-6.3-non-unique-arch
**Verdict:** ⚠ under-justified
**Quote (lines 1204–1208):** "The architectural decomposition r_k = a_k − b_k (additive form) is not uniquely determined — one can shift mass between a_k and b_k via any context-dependent function h(θ_<k, X_<k), since a_k and b_k share that context."
**Diagnosis:** Mathematically correct, but the "shift mass between" wording is slightly off. The shift is *additive* on both sides: a_k → a_k + h and b_k → b_k + h, leaving r_k = a_k − b_k unchanged. This preserves UMNN-monotonicity in θ_k (for a_k) and in X_k (for b_k), since h does not depend on θ_k or X_k. The UMNN parameterization can absorb h into the context-dependent bias term: bias(c) → bias(c) + h(c). So the shift preserves the architectural class. Verified: the only architectural constraint is monotonicity in the *primary* argument, and h is constant in the primary argument of both networks, so monotonicity is trivially preserved. The author's claim holds, but is slightly mis-stated as "shift mass between" rather than "shift both simultaneously".
**Suggested fix:** Reword: "...the shift a_k(θ_k; ·) ↦ a_k(θ_k; ·) + h(θ_<k, X_<k) and b_k(X_k; ·) ↦ b_k(X_k; ·) + h(θ_<k, X_<k) leaves r_k = a_k − b_k unchanged, and preserves UMNN-monotonicity of each component (since h is constant in the primary argument)."

### C-6.3-R2auto-role
**Verdict:** ⚠ under-justified
**Quote (lines 1213–1220):** "Without monotonicity in X_k at fixed (θ, X_<k), the 1D inductive step fails: X_k ↦ r_k could be non-bijective, and the conditional pushforward density would no longer be a monotone rearrangement."
**Diagnosis:** Correct in spirit but vague about *what specifically* fails. Two distinct things break without (R2^auto)'s monotonicity-in-X_k clause: (a) the conditional pushforward of X_k | θ, X_<k under r_k is no longer atomless / unique — the pushforward density picks up multiple-preimage contributions per Lemma 4.2's analysis, so the calibration constraint r_k | θ, X_<k ~ N(0,1) is no longer pinpointed to a unique monotone map; (b) the σ-algebra equivalence σ(θ, r_<k) = σ(θ, X_<k) used in the inductive step (see T-A-d diagnosis above) also requires (R2^auto) at *previous* levels, not just at level k. The remark conflates these. Also, the manuscript should clarify whether it means failure of the *dependence* part of (R2^auto) (r_k depending on X_>k) or failure of the *monotonicity* part. Both lead to failure, but in different ways:
- Loss of dependence restriction → ∂_X r not lower-triangular, Jacobian formula breaks (C-6.2-tri-jac fails).
- Loss of monotonicity → as remarked, non-bijective pushforward, Lemma 4.2's contradiction argument no longer pinpoints a unique r_k.

**Suggested fix:** Distinguish the two failure modes: "Without (R2^auto)'s X_k-monotonicity, Lemma 4.2's preimage-density contradiction at a critical point no longer rules out non-monotone r_k; the conditional calibration constraint can then be satisfied by multiple distinct r_k. Without (R2^auto)'s dependence restriction (r_k a function of X_{≤k} only), the Jacobian fails to be lower-triangular and the σ-algebra identity σ(θ, r_<k) = σ(θ, X_<k) used in the induction no longer holds."

### C-6.4-CI
**Verdict:** ✓ holds
**Quote (lines 1224–1229):** "C_α(X_obs) := {θ : ‖r(θ; X_obs)‖² ≤ χ²_{d,α}}. Under (C1), ‖r(θ_0; X)‖² | θ_0 ~ χ²_d, so C_α has exact frequentist coverage: P_{X~P_{θ_0}}(θ_0 ∈ C_α(X)) = α."
**Diagnosis:** Correct, with exact coverage at *every* θ_0 ∈ Θ (not merely ρ-a.e.). (C1) is defined in §1.3 as "for every θ_0 ∈ Θ" (line 263), so ‖r(θ_0; X)‖² ~ χ²_d at every θ_0, and the coverage probability is exactly α at every θ_0. Note: the proof of (C1) via NF-MLE in §3.2/§4 only establishes calibration ρ-a.e., not pointwise everywhere; the leap from "ρ-a.e. calibration" to "pointwise everywhere" requires continuity of r in θ (which holds under the architectural class) plus full ρ-support on the inferential region. This is a global question, not specific to §6.4 — see cross-Part finding below.

### C-6.4-CIalgo
**Verdict:** ✓ holds
**Quote (lines 1230–1233):** "The set C_α is found by numerical root-finding in θ: since r is monotone in θ (autoregressively), each 'slice' θ_k | θ_<k is a 1D root-finding problem."
**Diagnosis:** Correct. Given θ_<k, the function ‖r(θ; X_obs)‖² is built from (r_1(θ_1; ...), ..., r_d(θ_d; θ_<d, ...)), each strictly monotone in its own θ_k argument. So ‖r‖² as a function of θ_k (with all other components frozen) has known directional behavior at each level. Slice-by-slice 1D root-finding is valid. Algorithmically, the manuscript could spell out the order (presumably compute the θ_k-interval at fixed θ_<k by solving ‖r‖² = χ²_{d,α} for θ_k — but this is mildly non-trivial since the full ‖r‖² depends on all θ_k, not just one; the natural interpretation is autoregressive slice at fixed (θ_<k, θ_>k), but θ_>k cannot also be slice-conditioned). Minor exposition issue, not a correctness issue.

## Cross-Part findings

- **[Part I/III, C-1.3-C1 vs L-4.3]:** The (C1) assumption is defined as holding for *every* θ_0 ∈ Θ (§1.3), but L-4.3 only establishes "r(θ_0; X) | θ_0 ~ N(0,1) for ρ-a.e. θ_0". The gap from ρ-a.e. to everywhere relies on continuity in θ + full ρ-support, but this is never explicitly bridged. C-6.4-CI's "exact coverage at every θ_0" inherits this gap. Recommend either weakening C-6.4-CI to "for ρ-a.e. θ_0" or adding a continuity argument bridging the two.

- **[Part III, Theorem A's Lemma 4.2]:** T-A-d's induction reuses Theorem A "with F_k^{(θ)}(·|X_<k) playing the role of the Gaussian source". This silently extends Lemma 4.2 from Gaussian source to arbitrary C¹ atomless source with positive density on support. The extension is true (Lemma 4.2's proof only uses positivity of φ at the critical point), but should be noted in §4.2 or §6.3. Suggest adding a one-sentence remark after Lemma 4.2: "The same argument applies with any C¹ source density that is positive on the support of X — used in §6.3 for the conditional pushforward."

- **[Part V, §8.3 numerical check]:** §6.3's Cholesky corollary gives r_2^KR = 1.155(θ_2 − X_2) − 0.577(θ_1 − X_1) for Σ = [[1, 0.5],[0.5,1]]. §8.3 (E-8.3) claims "recovered E[J_θ] matches L^{-1} to 1–2%". The numerics in §6.3 are correct; consistency with E-8.3's claim should be verified in Part V review by checking that the recovered Jacobian's (2,1) entry is ≈ −0.577 and (2,2) entry is ≈ 1.155, with ∂_X r exactly the negative of ∂_θ r for the location family.
